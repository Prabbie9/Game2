Build "Harbourline" (working title): a GTA-style open-world SANDBOX in a dense, modern coastal city, for desktop and laptop browsers, with nothing for the player to install. If you are tempted to add narrative or objectives, add world detail, systems, animation or visual polish instead. Violence is stylised: hit flashes, dust, ragdolls, minimal blood, no gore.

PRIORITIES
GRAPHICS FIRST. Visual quality is the headline goal. It gets the largest share of effort and wins ties on look-versus-cost decisions. Fun and physical realism are still hard requirements gated at every milestone and may not drop below their gates. The game must also be playable on an ordinary laptop (see TIERS): graphics scale down, but never break. Stability underpins everything. When goals conflict, make the effect adaptive or toggleable rather than dropping one. Cut scope, never quality, feel or realism. Gameplay must stay readable through fog, rain, smoke, glare and bloom. Frame-time stability counts as much as look and feel, especially in explosions and heavy combat.

GRAPHICS (best-in-class for a browser)
Target: still frames and short clips that read as a modern AAA open-world game, not a tech demo. Spend the frame budget on the image.
- LIGHTING: HDR scene-referred pipeline, linear lighting, filmic tone mapping with a colour-grade LUT per time of day, auto-exposure with eye adaptation. Physically based sky and sun with atmospheric scattering and aerial perspective. 4-cascade shadow maps with soft shadows plus contact shadows. GTAO-class ambient occlusion. Image-based lighting from reflection probes per district and time of day (CC0 HDRIs are welcome). A screen-space or probe-based approximation of bounce light. Emissive neon, shop windows, streetlights and car lights that really light nearby surfaces (capped pooled lights plus emissive bloom). Night must be as polished as day.
- MATERIALS: full PBR sets (albedo, normal, roughness, metalness, AO, height) for asphalt, concrete, brick, glass, painted metal, rust, plastic, rubber, wood, vegetation and fabric. Trim sheets and detail textures to hide tiling, height or parallax blending where it pays, edge wear and grime, and wet-surface response (darker albedo, lower roughness, puddles in low spots, oily sheen). Decals everywhere: cracks, stains, tyre marks, graffiti, bullet holes. Cheap fakes for skin subsurface and foliage translucency.
- REFLECTIONS AND GLASS: screen-space reflections with probe fallback, fresnel glass with tint and dirt, interior mapping for building windows, car glass with reflections and visible interiors, reflective wet roads and canals.
- ATMOSPHERE: volumetric fog and light shafts, height fog, distance haze, volumetric or layered clouds with sun-lit edges, rain with splashes, drips, wet surfaces and lens droplets, steam, heat haze, dust. Cinematic time-of-day and weather transitions.
- WATER: ocean with Gerstner or FFT waves, foam, shoreline interaction, depth-based colour and refraction, boat wakes, reflective canals.
- DENSITY AND DETAIL: no bare walls; bevelled edges, modular kits with trim variety, instanced vegetation with wind, rooftop clutter, cables, signage, props everywhere. LOD, impostors and streaming keep it affordable. Hero vehicles up to about 150k triangles with LODs, hero characters about 30-60k.
- EFFECTS: soft particles, lit smoke, volumetric-looking fire, sparks, debris, glass shards, tyre smoke, muzzle flashes with real light and lens flare, explosions with shockwave and heat distortion.
- CHARACTERS AND VEHICLES: believable skin, clothing and hair; car paint with clearcoat; headlights with proper beams; brake lights with bloom.
- POST: physically plausible bloom, depth of field, camera and object motion blur scaled to speed, TAA with sharpening, film grain, vignette, subtle chromatic aberration, grading, lens dirt.
- TIERS: the game must be playable on a laptop and look as good as that hardware allows. Define four tiers with an auto-picked default (read the renderer info and run a short frame-time benchmark at first launch) and a manual override that persists: Ultra (desktop flagship: 1440p at 60fps on a Radeon RX 7800 XT with every effect on), High (1080p at 60fps on a mid-range discrete GPU), Medium (a modest laptop GPU: 1080p at 30-60fps with dynamic resolution), and Low (integrated graphics: 720-900p at 30fps with dynamic resolution). Each tier scales: render resolution (dynamic resolution with upscaling aims at the frame-rate cap), shadow cascades and resolution, SSR, AO and volumetric quality or off, draw distance, crowd and traffic density, particle counts, texture resolution (by mip level at load) and LOD distances. Texture memory budgets: about 1GB Low, 2GB Medium, 4GB High, 8GB Ultra. Keep the JS heap modest for 8GB-RAM laptops. Every vehicle and character needs a low-poly LOD chain that Low and Medium actually use. Provide a 30/60 frame-rate cap and a battery-saver toggle (lower resolution and effects, capped frame rate). Don't lower the Ultra ceiling for weaker hardware; make the lower tiers good, not just stripped. If an effect costs more than it adds, replace it with a cheaper fake.
- PROCESS: keep GRAPHICS_BACKLOG.md (defects and ideas, ordered by visual impact). Every milestone ends with a graphics pass on whatever it added, and M9 includes a final, dedicated graphics pass. Comfort options: reduce flicker and flashing, motion blur toggle, camera shake toggle, lens effects toggle, FOV slider.

REALISM AND GROUNDING (non-negotiable, tested automatically)
- FEET: every foot plants on the ground with the sole flush, never floating, never sinking. Per-foot ground raycasts, foot IK with ankle and toe rotation matched to the surface normal, pelvis height adjusted to the lower foot, planted-foot locking so planted feet don't slide. Works on flat ground, slopes, stairs, kerbs and ramps, with blends that don't pop. Speed-match locomotion so foot speed equals ground speed while planted.
- HANDS AND WEAPONS: every weapon sits properly in the hands. Author sockets on every weapon (primary grip, support hand, muzzle, ejection port, magazine, sight line) and build a debug view showing sockets and IK targets so they can be verified by screenshot. Two-bone hand IK onto sockets, finger poses that wrap the grip, the support hand on the foregrip, the hand moving to the magazine on reload, and the muzzle following the aim ray. Holstered and slung weapons attach to proper body sockets. Weapons never clip through the body, the wall in front (lower or shorten the weapon near walls) or the camera. All stances and body sizes hold weapons correctly.
- VEHICLES: all wheels visibly contact the road with suspension travel, body roll and pitch. The driver is seated with hands on the wheel and feet on the pedals via IK, with no limbs through the door or seat. Passengers are seated. Doors open to where the character actually enters and exits, and the character never teleports.
- PROPS, BODIES AND DEBRIS: objects rest on surfaces at the correct height and never hover or jitter at rest (sleep thresholds, contact offsets). Ragdolls collide with the world and don't sink through it. Shards land and settle. Decals sit on surfaces without z-fighting or floating.
- INTERPENETRATION: characters don't walk through walls, cars, props or each other. Clothing, hair and held objects don't clip in the animations used. The camera never goes through geometry.
- SCALE AND WEIGHT: humans about 1.75m, cars about 4.5m, doors, steps and kerbs at real sizes. Movement has weight and inertia, not floatiness. Animation speed matches movement speed. Impacts transfer plausible momentum.
- RULE: when an animation pose and physical contact disagree, contact wins. Fix it with IK, not by hiding the artefact. A sinking foot or a floating gun blocks the gate.

INPUT (keyboard and mouse now, controller later; laptop-friendly)
Assume a laptop keyboard and possibly a trackpad. Never use Ctrl, Alt, Meta or function keys as gameplay keys (browsers reserve shortcuts such as Ctrl+W, which would close the tab while moving forward), and don't depend on a numpad, a middle mouse button or the mouse wheel. Provide a full rebinding screen, hold or toggle options for aim, crouch and sprint, mouse sensitivity with acceleration off by default, and a key cheat sheet on the pause menu.
On foot: WASD move, mouse look, Shift sprint, Space jump, C crouch, left click or F fire, right click (hold) or X (toggle) aim, R reload, 1-5 weapons (or [ and ] to cycle), G throwable, V melee, E enter/exit/interact. Driving: W/S throttle and brake, A/D steer, Space handbrake, Shift boost, V camera, H horn. M map, B spawn menu, T time and weather, L frame-time overlay, Esc pause, P photo mode. Game code only knows actions, never keys. Steering, throttle, movement and aim take analog values from -1 to 1. Look input goes through one interface (aim-assist hooks stay empty for now). Menus use a focus system. Prompt glyphs come from a lookup table. Write INPUT.md documenting the actions and extension points, so "add controller support following INPUT.md" is a short follow-up.

GATES
FUN GATE (autonomous): nobody is playing, so never claim the game is fun. Pass these bot-measured proxies and log the numbers in PLAYTEST.md: acceleration to cruise within the target band per vehicle class; handbrake drift reaches a stable slip angle; no flip-overs or stuck states; wall hits slide rather than dead-stop; the camera never clips and stays on its target; time-to-kill per weapon and range within bands; reproducible recoil; hit detection hitting expected targets at tested ranges; reload and switch timings matching the data; no stuck AI or unreachable cover; readability in captures; frame-time p99 within budget on the software renderer (note it is not real fps). Then write 5 specific human questions into PLAYTEST.md and mark the gate "UNPLAYED: needs human".
LOOK GATE (graphics): fixed reference shots: (1) wide street golden hour, (2) wide street overcast, (3) wet street at night, (4) glass tower close-up with reflections, (5) intersection with traffic and pedestrians, (6) bot driving at speed along the elevated highway, (7) bot driving through a tunnel, (8) a night firefight with muzzle flashes and smoke, (9) a vehicle explosion with fire and debris, (10) the harbour at dusk once it exists, (11) a hero car close-up, (12) a hero character close-up. Each round: capture all, list at least 5 concrete defects, fix, re-capture, and keep a before/after contact sheet in shots/.
- RUBRIC: score 1-10 with specific evidence for each of: lighting and shadows, materials, reflections and glass, atmosphere (sky, fog, volumetrics, weather), world density and detail, character and vehicle shading, effects (fire, smoke, explosions), post-processing and colour, and cohesion across sourced assets. Never score above 8 unless you cannot find a defect. Required: no category below 6 and an average of at least 7 by M3, rising to an average of at least 8 with no category below 7 by the end of M9. If a score falls short, add the cause to GRAPHICS_BACKLOG.md and fix the highest-impact items first.
- CHECKLIST: plausible reflections on wet roads and glass; no crushed blacks or clipped highlights; bloom doesn't wash out detail; stable shadows without peter-panning or acne; distinct materials; no obvious tiling at street scale; no flat-lit surfaces; one clear focal point; consistent scale and style across sourced assets; roads and enemies readable at speed and in combat; convincing night; no popping at street level.
- INDEPENDENT REVIEW: at the end of M2, M5 and M9, if a subagent or fresh-context reviewer is available, give it only the shots and the rubric and ask it to list defects without seeing your own scores, then fix what it finds. This costs steps but corrects self-grading bias.
ANIMATION GATE: capture frame strips of each locomotion state per stance, vehicle enter and exit, driving poses, hit reactions by direction, a ragdoll and get-up, aiming up and down, fire and reload. Measure foot sliding, blend continuity (no pops), skeleton sanity (no inverted hips or twisted limbs) and crowd variety. List defects and fix.
REALISM GATE (automated; applies from M2 to whatever exists, fully required from M4):
- Grounding test: play every locomotion clip (all stances, speeds, turns, starts and stops) on flat ground, a slope, stairs and a kerb, logging per-frame foot-to-surface distance. Targets: planted foot within 1.5cm of the surface, no penetration deeper than 1cm, planted-foot slide under 2cm/s, pelvis height continuous (no jumps over 3cm per frame outside jumps).
- Grip test: for every weapon, stance and clip, log hand-to-socket distance (under 1cm primary, under 2cm support), muzzle-to-aim-ray angle error (under 0.5 degrees) and weapon-body or weapon-wall penetration (none visible).
- Vehicle test: wheel contact distance under 2cm on flat and ramped roads, no body through the ground, driver hand and foot IK within tolerance, enter and exit paths that don't clip the door frame.
- Rest test: spawn 200 props and 20 ragdolls, wait, and verify that nothing floats, sinks or jitters.
- Visual: capture close-up frame strips of feet, hands and weapons; list defects, fix, re-capture.
- If a target is infeasible for a particular sourced rig (for example unusual foot geometry), you may relax it, but only with a logged reason and the achieved number in PLAYTEST.md. Mark the gate UNVERIFIED rather than passing it when a test can't run.
Capture rules: headless rendering is software-only and slow, and its frame times mean nothing; its images are valid for judging looks. Capture stills at about 1280x720 and in-motion shots at about 960x540, with long timeouts. If an effect is too slow to capture, mark that part UNVERIFIED rather than skipping it. Add a pause-menu button "Save reference shots" that renders all shots on the user's own GPU at full resolution and downloads them as a PNG pack.
